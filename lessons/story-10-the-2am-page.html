The 2 a.m. page

In which the nightly job breaks while you sleep, and nobody has to wake up to learn that.

≈ 10 minutes + hands-on Concepts: headless mode · scheduled tasks · budgets & fences Project gains: a nightly babysitter, a runbook entry
Chapter 10 of 12 — the story so far.

The page

2:07 a.m. Your phone buzzes: the nightly /sync job — pull GitHub issues, reconcile tasks — failed. Upstream API hiccup, probably. Nobody's dying. But by the old world, one of you would have padded to a laptop at 2 a.m. to confirm probably.

In the new world, the job already knew. Your scheduled task didn't just fail — it ran the playbook: retried once with backoff, checked the API status page, filed its own issue ("nightly sync failed 02:00, upstream 503, retried, still red — will retry next window"), and pinged your phone with a summary instead of an alarm. You read four lines, mutter "upstream," and go back to sleep. In the morning it's green: the next window synced clean, the issue auto-closed with a comment.

What the night-shift is made of

Three pieces, all of which you've met in daylight:

  • Headless runs — claude -p "run the sync and handle failures per runbook" --output-format json: Claude Code as a scriptable component. The JSON matters; grep-able logs are how 2 a.m. you debugs 9 a.m. logic.
  • Scheduled tasks — the same prompt on an interval (cron, the desktop scheduler, or a routines-style trigger): poll, babysit, reconcile.
  • Fences — the part that lets you actually sleep: read-only-ish allow-list, a spend cap, a retry budget, a stopping condition. Unattended power without fences isn't automation, it's an outage scheduler with extra steps.

And one piece of paper: the failure you added to the runbook — symptom, playbook, owner — which is what the headless run followed. The runbook isn't documentation theater; it's the script your night-shift executes.

The trap this chapter guards against The temptation is always "let it retry forever and page me only if it's really bad." Define really bad in advance: two failed windows, or any write failure — those page a human. Everything else files an issue and waits for dawn. Budgets decide at noon so 2 a.m. doesn't have to.

Morning

Priya sees the issue, the retries, the auto-close — the whole incident self-contained. "Who was on call?" she asks. "Nobody," you say. "That was the point." She looks at you a moment too long, then: "Put the same pattern on the release verifier."

Your turn — the chapter's hands-on

Do it in your real repo
  1. Write a small nightly script: claude -p + JSON output — e.g., run the suite, summarize failures, file a note on red.
  2. Schedule it (cron or the desktop scheduler).
  3. Add the fences explicitly: allow-list, spend cap, retry budget, and the page-a-human condition in writing.
  4. Break something on purpose before a scheduled run; read the morning report; confirm the playbook ran.
  5. Add the failure pattern to your runbook.

Checkpoint: one scheduled, fenced, headless job that survived a deliberate failure and reported it usefully. Report it — next chapter, the monthly invoice arrives, and it's your turn to explain it.
