The bill

In which the invoice arrives, and you learn that watching costs is an engineering skill.

≈ 10 minutes + hands-on Concepts: usage visibility · spend limits · right-sizing · team settings Project gains: a cost dashboard habit, org-grade settings

The email

"Monthly usage is up 40%," says the subject line, and Priya forwards it with one word: "Explain." Not accusatory — worse: curious. You open the usage dashboard the way you'd open a suspicious package.

Forty minutes later you know three things you didn't:

  1. Where it went — usage broken down by workspace, by API key, by model, by day. The spike: your Chapter 10 night-shift, plus Dana's ultrareviews.
  2. What it costs — cached vs uncached input in the report tells the real story: your long nightly contexts were re-billed full-price because the prompt changed every run. Stable prefix → cache reads at a tenth. One refactor of the nightly prompt ordering and the line item drops by half.
  3. What it should be — right-sizing: the sync job and the diff reviewer don't need the big model for every call. Chores step down a tier; deep review stays big. Same lesson as the exam course's cost chapter, now with your own name on the invoice.

You write the explanation as three bullet points and one fix each. Priya replies "good" — the third one — and CC's finance with a spend limit for the workspace. The limit isn't distrust; it's a fence so the next surprise pages a budget instead of a person.

The quiet upgrade

While you're in admin territory, you finish the team's growing-pains work: a managed settings layer — org-level policy (allow-lists, the spend caps, hook rules) that travels to every dev's machine and every CI runner, and that nothing below it overrides. Your Chapter 7 guardrails stop being "in our repo" and become "how Meridian works." You also switch on usage analytics for the team — the consumption plane from your lifecycle lesson, wired: who, what, how much, trending where.

What just happened, named Costs became observable, explained, and fenced: usage by key/model/day; the cache as a design lever; right-sizing as habit; spend limits as fences; managed settings as policy at fleet scale. The bill stopped being weather and became architecture.

Your turn — the chapter's hands-on

Do it in your real repo
  1. Open your usage view (console workspace, analytics dashboard, or OTel export if you wired it); find your top cost driver by day and by cause.
  2. Apply one lever: stable-prefix caching on your longest recurring prompt, a model step-down on one chore, or /clear discipline between unrelated tasks.
  3. Set or propose one spend limit, in writing, with what it pages.
  4. Sketch the org settings chain: managed → repo → local, one line each on what lives where.

Checkpoint: a explained cost driver, one applied lever with before/after numbers, one fence proposed. Report it — the last chapter is next, and it ends with a package, not a page.
