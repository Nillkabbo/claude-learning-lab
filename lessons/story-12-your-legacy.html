Chapter 12 of 12 — the story so far.

Six months later

Meridian has eleven developers now. Tracker is on every machine: add, list, close, tag, search — born from your first Monday, hardened by a quote character, guarded by rules you wrote after a near-miss nobody remembers but you. And there's a new hire — bright, eager, asks the exact question Marco asked on your first week: "How do I run this?"

You could answer. But you've learned what Priya knew on a whiteboard a lifetime ago: anything explained twice becomes documentation. Except you can go further than she meant — because everything you built this year isn't scattered know-how anymore. It's packageable.

The package

Over the year, your repo accumulated an arsenal: the /release skill, the /sync skill, the read-only detective, the audit hook, the guardrail settings, the night-shift script. A plugin bundles exactly this — skills, agents, hooks, config — into one installable, namespaced unit (meridian:release, meridian:detective…) that a team shares via a marketplace with one command. So that's the finale's build: the Meridian Toolkit.

You inventory what ships (and cut what shouldn't — the audit hook is Meridian policy, org-managed, not a plugin), write the manifest, publish to the team marketplace, and when the new hire asks the question, Marco — Marco — answers first:

"Run /plugin install meridian/toolkit. Read CLAUDE.md. Then ask Claude — it knows this place."

v1.0

tracker add "ship v1.0"
tracker list          # the whole year, in twelve lines of closed tasks
/release v1.0

The suite runs green — it has for eleven straight releases. The changelog reads like your story: skeleton… quotes… atomic writes… close… tags, search… Priya reads it end to end, closes her laptop, and says the sentence this whole story was built to earn:

"Who's ready to build the next one?"

What you now hold — the entire journey, inventoried A complete project: Tracker — real CLI, real tests, real CI, real releases, real users. A shipped toolkit: skills, a subagent, automation, guardrails — packaged. And the capability inventory behind it all: sessions and modes (ch1–2), memory (ch3), skills (ch4), subagents (ch5), MCP with trust review (ch6), enforcement layers (ch7), parallel work (ch8), the review stack (ch9), headless automation (ch10), costs and fleet policy (ch11), packaging (ch12). That is the whole of Claude Code — not read, lived.

Your turn — the final hands-on

The finish line
  1. Audit your toolkit: list every skill, agent, hook, and setting your repo accumulated. Cut what's project-specific; keep what's team-valuable.
  2. Package it as a plugin: manifest, marketplace (even a one-person one), install-from-fresh test — the new-hire simulation.
  3. Ship Tracker v1.0 via your release skill. Full suite green. Tag pushed.
  4. Write the project's README as the story's epilogue: what it is, what it taught, what's next.

Final checkpoint — the goal, complete: one entire project (working CLI, tests, CI, releases, docs), one shipped toolkit, one fresh-machine install proving a stranger could start where you started and be productive in an hour. Report it to your agent — that report is the graduation. Then, if the certification is still calling: everything in this story maps one-to-one onto the exam's six scenarios, and the architect course plus the mocks are warmed up and waiting.
